To structure a DJ set is to decide, before you play, how the energy of the room
moves from your first track to your last: where it starts, how it climbs, where
it peaks and how it lands. The tracks are the material. The structure is the
order they go in, and the order is a shape you can draw — an
[energy curve](/glossary/energy-curve) — and check.

That is the whole idea, and it is worth saying plainly because most advice on
this topic is about *picking* tracks. Picking is the fun part and you are
probably already good at it. Structure is what you do with twenty-five tracks
you have already chosen and an hour to fill.

## Start from the slot, not the tracks

The shape of a set is decided by when you are playing, before you have picked a
single record. An opening slot has a different job from a peak-time slot, and a
closing slot has a different job again — and the difference is not "faster or
slower" but where the energy is allowed to go.

- **A [warm-up](/glossary/warm-up) set** builds a floor. Its energy starts low
  and climbs slowly, and its biggest mistake is a peak: play the track that
  belongs at one in the morning at eleven, and the DJ after you has nowhere to
  go.
- **A [peak-time](/glossary/peak-time) set** holds the room at the top. It still
  moves — a flat hour at maximum energy is exhausting, not exciting — but it
  moves *around* a high level rather than climbing toward one.
- **A [closing set](/glossary/closing-set)** brings the night down without
  dropping it. The energy falls, but by choice and gradually, and the last
  track is a decision rather than whatever was left.

Write the slot down first. Every other choice in this guide is made against it.

## Draw the arc, then fill it

With the slot decided, the arc is the picture of where energy should be at each
point in the set. A warm-up arc rises. A peak-time arc is high with movement. A
closing arc falls. You do not need software to draw one — a line on paper with
"start" at one end and "end" at the other will do — but you do need to draw it,
because a set structured without an arc is a set structured by accident.

Then fill it. Take your tracks, give each one an energy value — the
[1 to 10 scale](/glossary/track-energy) most DJs already use, from a tag or
from your own ear — and place them along the arc where their energy belongs. A
[build-up](/glossary/build-up) goes where the arc climbs; a
[breakdown](/glossary/breakdown) goes where it needs air.

This is the moment the structure becomes visible. Tracks that felt right in the
abstract turn out to have nowhere to go, and gaps appear where the arc wants a
step you do not own.

## The three checks

Once the tracks are placed, the order can be checked, and these are the three
checks worth running on every set. They are the same ones
[the article on whether your set is in the right order](/blog/is-my-dj-set-in-the-right-order)
goes through in more detail.

**Does the energy move without jolts?** Read the values in play order. A climb
of one or two points at a time is a build; a jump of four is a lurch, and a
lurch downward mid-set is the one the floor notices. There is no single right
size for a step — [how much is too much depends on the moment](/blog/how-much-energy-jump-is-too-much-dj)
— but a step you cannot explain is a step to look at.

**Do neighbouring tracks mix?** Two tracks in a row that fight harmonically make
a transition that has to be hidden rather than played. Check the keys with the
[Camelot wheel](/glossary/camelot-wheel): compatible neighbours make the
structure playable, not just plausible on paper. The rules of
[harmonic mixing](/glossary/harmonic-mixing) are the cheapest structural fix
there is, because they change nothing about which tracks you play — only which
ones sit next to each other.

**Do the peaks land where the slot needs them?** Go back to the arc. Is the
highest-energy track where the arc is highest? If the biggest moment of a
warm-up set is at the midpoint, the set has a peak-time structure in a warm-up
slot, and it will read that way from the floor.

## The mistakes a curve shows you

Some structural mistakes are invisible in a tracklist and obvious in a drawing.
These are the ones that come up most.

- **The early peak.** The best track goes in too soon, because it is the best
  track and you want to play it. Everything after it is a comedown you did not
  plan.
- **The plateau.** Six tracks at the same energy in a row. Individually fine,
  together a flat line, and a flat line is the shape of a room losing interest.
- **The sawtooth.** Up, down, up, down — usually the result of ordering by
  what mixes rather than by where the set is going. Each transition is clean
  and the set has no direction.
- **The cliff.** A drop of several points in one step, almost always a track
  that belongs to a different set altogether.

None of these are fixed by picking different tracks. All of them are fixed by
moving the ones you have.

## Check it before you play it

Everything above can be done with a pen. It can also be done in a minute with
the [free energy-curve tool](/tools/energy-curve): export the playlist from
your DJ software, drop it in, and the curve is drawn from the tags you already
have — including the energy values, if your files carry them, and the keys.
Nothing about the file leaves your browser.

If what you want is the opposite — a set built *for* you from your library and
a chosen vibe, rather than a check on one you built — that is a different kind
of tool, and [SetFlow's own description of itself](/compare/setflow) is the
honest answer to whether it is the one you need.

```faq
Q: How do I structure a DJ set if I don't know the slot?
A: Ask. If nobody can tell you, plan for the middle: an arc that rises, holds, and eases — it survives being moved earlier or later better than a set built for one specific hour. Then leave two or three tracks at each end unplanned so you can adjust on the night.

Q: Does every set need a peak?
A: No. A warm-up set is structured precisely so that it does not have one — its job is to leave the peak for the next DJ. Structure means the energy goes where the slot needs it, and for some slots that means never reaching the top.

Q: Can I structure a set that has no BPM or key tags?
A: Yes. The arc is about energy, and energy can be judged by ear and written down as a number. Keys and BPM make the harmonic check possible, but a set with neither can still be ordered by its shape — and tagging the files later only makes the second check available, not the first.
```
