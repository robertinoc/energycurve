A DJ set is a continuous performance in which a DJ plays a sequence of tracks —
usually other people's records — joined together so the music does not stop,
in an order chosen to give the room a direction. It happens live, to an
audience, within a slot of the night, and it is judged less by any single
track than by the shape the whole sequence makes: its
[energy curve](/glossary/energy-curve).

That definition has four parts, and each one separates a set from something it
is often confused with.

## Continuous — which is what separates it from a playlist

A playlist is a list. A set is a performance of one, and the difference is the
joins. In a set, each track overlaps the next and the DJ makes that overlap
sound like a single piece of music: tempos matched, phrases aligned, keys
compatible where they can be. Press play on a playlist and you get tracks with
gaps between them. Play a set and the gaps are the work.

The mechanics of those joins are their own subject —
[how a DJ set works](/blog/how-does-a-dj-set-work) goes through them — but the
short version is that a set is a playlist somebody is *playing*, rather than
one that is merely being played.

## Ordered — which is what separates it from a crate

The tracks are not simply the DJ's favourites in any order. They are placed so
that the energy of the night moves somewhere: it might climb, it might hold, it
might ease down, but it goes somewhere on purpose. That order is the set's
structure, and it is decided before the gig far more than it is improvised at
it. [How to structure a DJ set](/blog/how-to-structure-a-dj-set) is the
practical guide; the point here is only that the order is part of what a set
*is*, not a detail of how one is played.

## Live, in a slot — which is what separates it from a mix

A DJ mix is usually a recorded product: a podcast, a radio hour, something
built on a timeline and rendered to a file that can be edited afterwards. A set
is played once, to a room, and cannot. That is not a small difference. It means
a set is planned to survive contact with an audience — a track swapped when the
floor thins, a peak brought forward when it fills — and a mix is planned to be
listened to alone.

The tools reflect the split. Software like DJ.Studio exists to build the
recorded kind, on a timeline, with the audio rendered at the end; set-planning
tools exist to order the live kind and hand the order back to the booth. The
[comparison with DJ.Studio](/compare/dj-studio) is largely a comparison
between these two things.

## The kinds of set, by slot

Because a set belongs to a slot, sets come in kinds, and the kind decides the
shape.

- **The [warm-up](/glossary/warm-up) set** opens the night. It builds a floor
  and deliberately withholds the peak, which belongs to whoever plays next.
- **The [peak-time](/glossary/peak-time) set** is the centre of the night,
  played to a full room at the top of its energy. It still moves — it has to —
  but it moves around a high level rather than climbing toward one.
- **The [closing set](/glossary/closing-set)** brings the night down. Its
  energy falls by choice, and its last track is a decision rather than a
  leftover.
- **The [b2b](/glossary/b2b) set** is two or more DJs alternating, usually a
  track or two at a time. Its shape is negotiated live, which is what makes it
  harder to plan and, when it works, more alive than either DJ alone.

How long a set is depends entirely on the slot, the venue and the bill; there
is no standard length, and a set is not more of a set for being longer.

## What makes a DJ set good

Not the tracks, or not only. A good set is one whose order does what its slot
needs: the energy moves without unexplained lurches, the peaks land where the
night wants them, and neighbouring tracks sit well enough together that the
joins can be played rather than hidden. A set of excellent records in the wrong
order is a worse set than a set of good records in the right one, and every DJ
has played both.

That is also why a set can be checked before it is played. The shape is a
matter of order and energy, and both are known in advance — which is what
[the free energy-curve tool](/tools/energy-curve) draws from a playlist
exported from your DJ software, without the file leaving your browser.

```faq
Q: Is a DJ set the same as a DJ mix?
A: No. A set is played live, to a room, in a slot, and cannot be edited afterwards. A mix is typically recorded — a podcast or radio hour — built on a timeline and rendered to a file. The joins are similar; everything around them is different.

Q: How long is a DJ set?
A: As long as the slot. Opening, peak-time and closing sets differ in length as much as in shape, and festival, club and radio slots differ again. There is no standard, and length says nothing about quality.

Q: Do you need your own music to play a DJ set?
A: No. A DJ set is, by definition, mostly other people's records — that is what distinguishes DJing from a live act. What is yours is the selection and the order, and those are the whole craft.
```
