# The name collision, and what to do about it

**Decision: keep the name. Stop trying to own the bare phrase.**

`docs/seo-aeo-baseline-2026-08.md` measured the collision and named it correctly:
`energycurve.com` is an agritech company and it takes every slot on the branded
query. This document doesn't repeat that. It adds the two things the baseline
didn't check, and they point at a different problem than the one we thought we had.

## What the baseline didn't check

### 1. The other company is in a different industry, and has no claim on the words

Energy Curve of Archie, Missouri sells **biological fertilizer and plant
nutrition** — Benefit Hay, Benefit Grow, Benefit Backgrounding. Hay, cattle, row
crops, sugar beets.

Their registered marks are the **BENEFIT family** (`BENEFIT HAY®`,
`BENEFIT FATTENING®`, `BENEFIT DIGEST®`). A search turned up **no registration
for "Energy Curve" itself**, by them or anyone.

**The caveat, stated plainly: a web search is not a trademark clearance search.**
I am not a lawyer and this is not advice. If you want certainty before spending
money on the brand, that is a few hundred dollars of a trademark attorney's time
and it is the only way to get it. What the search does establish is that there is
no *obvious* registration to trip over, and that fertilizer and DJ software are
different classes of goods — trademark protection is per class, and nobody
confuses a bag of soil amendment with a set-analysis app.

**So the legal risk is low and the discovery problem is real.** Those are
different problems and only one of them is worth spending on.

### 2. The actual collision is not the .com. It's that our name is a category noun

This is the finding that changes the decision. "Energy curve" is not our phrase,
and it never was — it is **the DJ category's own vocabulary**, and competitors
ship features named with it:

| Product | What they call it |
|---|---|
| Djify | "energy curve editor" |
| SetFlow | "five classic energy curve shapes" |
| HarmonySet | "BPM curve", "energy modes" |
| Mixgraph | "Energy Arc Planner" |
| Mixed In Key | "Energy Level" (per track, 1–10) |

The baseline already noted that "energy flow" and "energy arc" outrank "energy
curve" in usage. It's worse than a synonym problem: our brand **is** the generic
term for the thing the whole category does. Every competitor's blog post about
energy curves is a competitor for our branded query, and so is every DJ forum
thread. The fertilizer company is the least of it.

## What that means, both ways

**In our favour, and it's not small.** The name needs no explanation to the
audience. A DJ reads "EnergyCurve" and knows what it does before the page loads.
That is worth more than most brands buy with a naming exercise, and a renamed
product would have to spend to earn it back.

**Against us.** A descriptive name is weak: hard to register, impossible to stop
others from using, and unwinnable as a search term. We will never own "energy
curve", and effort spent trying is effort not spent on queries we can win.

## The decisions

1. **Keep the name.** The legibility is worth more than the search head term, and
   renaming costs the one asset we have — that people who see it understand it.

2. **`energycurve.app` is canonical, permanently.** Never chase the `.com`. It
   belongs to a going concern with a dealer network and forty years of history;
   it is not for sale at a price this makes sense at, and an offer would only
   tell them it has value.

3. **Own the compound, not the phrase.** They write it as two words ("Energy
   Curve"), we write it as one ("EnergyCurve") — that is already true in the logo
   and every page title, and it is the only part of the string we can plausibly
   own. Keep it rigid: one word, no space, everywhere.

4. **Never bid on or optimise for the head term.** "Energy curve" as a query is
   a fertilizer company, five competitors' content, and generic DJ advice. The
   five Spanish articles already point at the right target — question-shaped
   long-tail where nothing ranks. That is the whole strategy and it needs no
   change from this document.

5. **Always ship the disambiguator.** Already the case in title tags; worth
   keeping as a rule rather than a habit, because it is the one thing that makes
   `energycurve dj` and `energycurve app` reachable.

## What would change this

- **They register "Energy Curve" broadly**, or in a class covering software. Then
  this becomes a legal question rather than an SEO one, and the answer might be
  a rename.
- **They move into audio or software.** Vanishingly unlikely for a company whose
  Vision Platform is crop nutrient management, but it is the trigger.
- **We want defensible IP** — for a raise, an acquisition, or to stop a
  competitor. A descriptive mark won't provide it, and that is the one scenario
  where renaming is the right answer rather than an expensive way to lose
  comprehension.

Until one of those happens, this is settled and needs no further work.
